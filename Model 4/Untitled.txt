Model 4: Spending Behavior Clustering & Segmentation
Main Objective

The core objective of this model is to understand users’ financial habits and categorize them into distinct behavioral groups. Unlike simple expense categorization (which looks at individual transactions) or credit score prediction (which predicts a numerical score), this model looks at patterns over time.

It answers questions like:

“Which users are high spenders vs. moderate spenders vs. savers?”

“What are the most common spending trends among different types of users?”

“How can we provide personalized insights and recommendations based on behavior rather than single transactions?”

In short: it gives the platform a ‘user behavior intelligence layer’.

What This Model Does

Aggregates and Analyzes Spending Data:

Takes in transaction history (amount, category, date, frequency, etc.) and calculates features like monthly spending, spending per category, recurring expenses, and discretionary spending.

Identifies Patterns:

Uses unsupervised learning (like K-Means clustering) to find natural groupings in the data.

For example, two users might spend the same total amount, but one spends mostly on essentials, while the other spends heavily on entertainment. Clustering captures these differences.

Segments Users:

Assigns each user to a cluster representing a typical spending behavior.

Clusters could be named for easier interpretation, e.g., “Conservative Saver,” “Balanced Spender,” “High Risk/High Spend.”

Supports Personalized Recommendations:

Helps the Investment Recommendation System by providing context: e.g., “This user is a high spender; suggest safer investments with short-term gains” vs. “This user is a saver; suggest long-term growth options.”

Enables alerts and insights tailored to user behavior, like “You’re trending toward overspending this month” or “You’ve saved 30% more than users in your cluster.”

Predictive and Preventive Capability:

Over time, the model can predict behavioral shifts, alerting users before bad habits form (e.g., rising discretionary spending that could affect savings).

Contribution to the Whole Project

Think of SmartFin as a full-stack financial intelligence system. Here’s how Model 4 fits in:

Feature/Model	Role in Project	Contribution of Clustering Model
Expense Categorization	Labels each transaction	Provides raw data for understanding patterns

✅ In simple terms:
This model transforms transaction-level data into user-level behavioral intelligence, giving SmartFin the ability to know its users, not just their numbers. It’s what turns a simple finance app into an AI-driven personal finance advisor.
